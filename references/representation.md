# Represent the named subject, not automatically a teaching prop

Resolve WHAT is being represented before asset search or geometry. Default `representationMode: real-subject`: depict the actual subject in its appropriate natural/physical form. “Educational”, “explain”, “3D model”, “diorama” or a toon/pixel style does not authorize replacing it with a toy, classroom aid, bench apparatus or museum display.

## Three modes; delegate only when asked

- **real-subject**: the actual thing. Earth is a planet, a heart is the organ, a volcano is terrain. A real machine retains its actual feet/housing; natural objects do not gain an invented base, stand, spindle or mechanism. Scale compression is declared; no false claim of literal size.
- **teaching-model**: a physical demonstrator deliberately representing the thing: a classroom globe, orrery, mounted anatomical model or tabletop rig. Use only when explicitly requested/confirmed or explicitly delegated with a stated reason. Its supports and mechanisms need plausible construction but must not be confused with how the represented natural subject works.
- **explanatory**: an openly simplified visualization: section, exploded layout, field/flow arrows, diagrammatic scale or symbolic geometry. Keep subject identity and correct causal explanation. A cutaway or overlay can also be a view of real-subject; it does not require turning the subject into a tangible toy.

A “realistic classroom globe” is still teaching-model; “stylized Earth in space” is still real-subject. An imported asset's form must match the mode: a commercially licensed globe on a stand cannot substitute for a requested planet just because both have the right title. Asset accuracy/role and representation are separate checks.

## Ask only for a material unresolved choice

If the user explicitly names the form, follow it. If they simply ask to explain the subject, use real-subject without another question. The word “model” alone is not an instruction for a physical scale model.

If the brief genuinely leaves competing forms unresolved, ask once:

> **How should I represent the subject?**
> 1. **The real thing** — actual subject in its appropriate setting.
> 2. **A physical teaching model** — a tangible demonstrator with intentional supports.
> 3. **An explanatory visualization** — simplified sections, fields or exploded forms.
> 4. **You choose** — choose the form that best serves the explanation and state why.

Combine with an already-needed style question where possible. Don't ask again during repairs or per component. If a repair's old design silently substituted a globe/toy for an actual-subject request, correct that mismatch rather than preserving an erroneous surrogate as a supposed prior choice.

## Keep independent decisions independent

- **Render preset:** pixel / toon / hybrid changes visual treatment, not subject identity.
- **Presentation:** full scene / standalone / both controls surrounding context. Standalone Earth is still a planet, not automatically a globe. Do not force this extra question or implement a dual-mode toggle unless requested or meaningfully unresolved.
- **Exterior / Inside:** authored explanatory views, not permission to change the subject into a classroom object. Preserve its complete default form unless an explicit permanent section was requested.
- **Educational profile:** causal text/lessons and inspection behavior, not mandatory legs, casing, platform or a three-part machine analogy for everything.

Record mode and decision origin (`explicit`, `default`, `clarified`, `delegated`) plus one-line rationale in DESIGN. Keep the user's mode during normal repair. Reference/asset searches include the actual form/variant; layout proof checks for unwanted stands, floors, display bases or toy mechanisms before detail. Add explanatory layers to the subject rather than replacing the subject to make labelling easier.

## Small semantic regression set

Expected behavior: “explain Earth”, “3D model of Earth in space”, “pixel volcano”, “standalone beating heart” → real-subject, no representation question or invented display mount. “Classroom globe with stand”, “anatomical teaching model on a plinth” → teaching-model. “Diagrammatic exploded pump”, “schematic Earth layer visualization” → explanatory. “Cutaway of the real pump” → real-subject with a cutaway, not automatically a physical demonstrator. An exhibit brief explicitly undecided between a planet and tabletop globe → clarify. Repair of an explicitly chosen teaching model → retain teaching-model. Style/base preferences alone do not resolve or override these choices.

These are instruction/semantic-consumer fixtures, not an automatic scientific-truth detector or proof of a rendered scene. No Earth scene is commissioned by these examples.
